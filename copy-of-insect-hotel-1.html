REVISITED

permanent public sculpture Insect Hotel Revisited in collaboration with rasmus strange - rasmusthue.com It’s so exciting to return to a work after a few years. Time is, after all, one of the work’s most important materials. Four years later, the Douglas fir has developed its characteristic silvery-grey patina, mirroring the reflections of the stainless-steel rods. The sculpture is oriented towards north, south, east and west, allowing the path of the sun and the changing seasons to become an active part of the work through light, shadows, and the slow transformation of its materials. 1274 Rooms Vacant functions as an insect hotel and is dedicated to the 1,274 threatened or extinct pollinators in Denmark. Situated within a circular beech hedge and surrounded by a grid of apple trees, the sculpture acts as a catalyst for the landscape processes that connect pollinators, vegetation, and people. The work is therefore both a monument to the biodiversity crisis and a tangible contribution to the ecosystem of which it forms part. Over time, it becomes less an object and more a part of the landscape’s cycles. 1274 light holes vacant / 350 metal rods in collaboration with rasmus strange - rasmusthue.com In Denmark, we have a 'red list assessment' of the species found in Danish nature. Each species is assessed in relation to the level of threat, where a distinction is made between 'viable' and 'regionally extinct, critically endangered, endangered, vulnerable and almost endangered'. A species is thus' red-listed 'if it falls outside the' viable 'category'. The work is based on the group of pollinating insects: bees, day butterflies and night butterflies. Their pollination is absolutely central to the well-being of ecosystems and also makes them particularly vulnerable, which is why bees and day butterflies in particular are under pressure. This is largely due to monoecology, pesticides, habitat destruction, and hence shortages of nesting and overwintering. The total number of assessed species of bees, day and night butterflies, is 1274. 924 (73.5%) are viable, but 350 (27.5%) are red-listed. The work consists of two parallel walls, stacked of oak timber, leaning against each other. Each wall is pierced with 1274 holes - one hole for each species. Here the light can shine through and an insect can live or overwinter. 350 of the holes are plugged with one rod - one rod for each of the red-listed species.
